Methodology & limitations

GeoSafe-FIS retrieves available official hazard classifications through verified GeoRisk Philippines ULAP services, then keeps those source values separate from its explainable model transformations.

The score supports early planning conversations. It does not replace detailed geotechnical, hydrologic, structural, land-use, or emergency-management studies.

How an assessment is produced

  1. 01
    Select and identify

    The point is checked against the configured Basey municipal and barangay sources, with provenance preserved.

  2. 02
    Retrieve live evidence

    The backend queries verified ULAP services and records the raw code, official label, status, layer, attribution, retrieval time, and cache state.

  3. 03
    Transform transparently

    A configured GeoSafe-FIS normalization converts an available official label into a separate internal model value and fuzzy memberships.

  4. 04
    Evaluate rules

    Configured rules are activated, weighted, aggregated, and preserved with their activation strengths.

  5. 05
    Explain the result

    A 0–100 score and descriptive category are presented with the evidence, rules, missing-data status, and cautious recommendations.

Responsible interpretation

Data availability and quality

01

Missing stays missing

A required unavailable hazard input makes an assessment incomplete; it is never converted to zero or low vulnerability.

02

Provenance travels with results

Provider, date, processing notes, availability, and quality notices remain visible in both the interface and report.

03

Live or cached is explicit

Every hazard value shows its source URL, retrieval time, cache source and expiry. Stale values are labelled and never silently reused.

Required disclaimer

Limitations and appropriate use

  • Results depend on the spatial resolution, currency, completeness, and classification of the available datasets.
  • Boundary and hazard overlays may contain positional uncertainty and should not be interpreted as cadastral or parcel-level delineations.
  • A complete three-hazard score is blocked when flood, liquefaction, or a verified ground-shaking value is unavailable.
  • Historical incident records describe known records only; gaps may reflect limited reporting rather than absence of past events.
  • CLUP references are contextual planning information and must be verified against the currently adopted official plan and ordinances.
  • Fuzzy scores are relative screening outputs determined by the configured membership functions and rules, not probabilities or forecasts.
  • Recommendations are cautious planning prompts, not engineering designs, legal advice, evacuation instructions, or approval decisions.

Disclaimer

This report is a preliminary multi-hazard screening output based on selected available data. It does not certify that a location is safe or unsafe and does not replace official hazard, planning, engineering, geological, geotechnical, or regulatory assessment.
